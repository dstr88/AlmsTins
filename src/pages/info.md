Charlie Brown Puppets:

Woodstock

Bunny

Six Little Birds

Butterfly

​

Woodstock was created by using wooden reeds to form the base and other pieces of wood and metal, then filled in by foam and coated in foam clay and wood filler. Moving parts include the legs, wings, and head. 

​

Bunny is made of heat shaped foam and zip ties, with wooden dowels as handles and finished off with paint. Movement is mainly in the upper body segment and head, but tail also moves.








Puppet Design

Curse of the Loveless Girl Puppets:

Villan

Angler Fish

Bear 

​​

The Villian was created through cardboard and wood, with found items for detailing such as broken forks for the thorns and papermache for the texture. Movement was performed through the actor wearing the tall shoulder gear and head piece and moving the wooden poles on which the hands were fixed.

​

The  Angler Fish was created purely through cardboard and paper matche with a wire to hold a small light. Movement was in the jaw controlled through a simple handle.

​

The Bear was a cardboard and wood creation aimed in design at a simple geometric breakdown, and the body was created by just a piece of fabric and three actors.


Costumes







The costumes for Big River held great meaning in the colors for each scene, from being faded at beginning, to full color at end to symbolize character development.  In addition, the main characters, Huck Finn and Jim have the color olive, which is special to them and is not used for anyone else in the play.  Other colors such as purple and red are also assigned to characters to connect and create a specific trait, along with historical color meaning.  

hese Shining Lives was a student led production at Samford University.  Colors, patterns, and collars played a huge role in these costumes, and this play was made to be as historically accurate as possible with little resources available.

The Lion the Witch and the Wardrobe used subtle hints to the character's traits, without overdoing the animal appearance.

For Antigone (a school project), I designed the set and costumes after Star Wars designs and created a unique world for this classic play.

My Artist Philosophy
I believe art is the hidden language for the soul and that is vital for every single mind to witness and participate in artistic expressions.  Storytelling is one of my favorite details to incorporate subtly into costumes and sets that complete the story without someone even realizing the importance of the detail.  As much as art is my personal endeavor, I love the community it builds, and how so many different minds can create for one goal, whether that be theatre productions, dance performances, or animated stories.  I believe God has led me to use my art to connect people and stories.  I have found costuming is truly an important factor in storytelling, and that it is truly important to who I am as an artist.  My mom and my grandmother are both huge inspirations, through how my grandmother raised my mom to raise me to try new things every day.  She would create dresses and costumes for my mom and aunt anytime, and always strived for the adventure of new things.  Art, dance, and costuming have opened my eyes to the endless possibilities of creativity, and I hope to be able to share this with others through what I love and how I express myself.
